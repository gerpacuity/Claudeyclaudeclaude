# Business Case: Regrading of HIQA CEO from Assistant Secretary to Deputy Secretary

## Prepared for the Board of the Health Information and Quality Authority

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This business case sets out the justification for regrading the Chief Executive Officer of the Health Information and Quality Authority (HIQA) from the **Assistant Secretary** equivalent grade to the **Deputy Secretary** equivalent grade within the Irish civil/public service pay framework.

Since HIQA's establishment under the Health Act 2007, its regulatory remit, operational complexity, staff complement, budget, and national significance have expanded dramatically. The CEO grade has not kept pace with these changes. Benchmarking against comparable domestic state bodies demonstrates that HIQA is now an outlier: the heads of bodies with comparable or lesser scope, complexity, and public accountability are already remunerated at Deputy Secretary level or above.

**The current grading is anomalous, unsustainable, and risks HIQA's ability to attract and retain leadership of the calibre required.**

## 3. HIQA: Scope, Scale and Complexity of Role

### 3.1 Statutory Mandate

HIQA was established under the **Health Act 2007** as an independent authority with responsibility for:

- Setting standards for health and social care services
- Monitoring and inspecting healthcare and social care services
- Regulating residential services for older people, people with disabilities, and children
- Conducting health technology assessments (HTA) and evidence synthesis
- Advising the Minister for Health and the HSE
- Overseeing health information quality and standards

### 3.2 Regulatory Scope (2024-2026) - Unprecedented Expansion

HIQA's remit has expanded substantially beyond its original 2007 mandate:

| Regulatory Function | Scale |
|---|---|
| **Designated centres for older persons** (nursing homes) | ~580 registered centres |
| **Designated centres for persons with disabilities** | ~1,400+ registered centres |
| **Children's services** | Statutory children's residential centres, special care units, Oberstown |
| **Public hospitals** | All public acute and community hospitals |
| **Private hospitals** (from September 2024) | 23 additional hospitals (Patient Safety Act 2023) |
| **Total healthcare providers monitored** | 116+ services |
| **Inspections conducted (2024)** | 869+ centres visited; 949+ inspections |
| **International Protection Accommodation Centres** (from January 2024) | New function |
| **Health Technology Assessment** | National HTA function, EU HTA Regulation participation |

### 3.3 Imminent Further Expansion

The CEO must now prepare for and lead the organisation through three further major expansions:

1. **Patient Safety (Licensing) Bill** - HIQA will become the **licensing authority for all public and private hospitals** in Ireland, with statutory powers to grant, amend, or revoke hospital licences. This is a transformational change making HIQA the single most consequential healthcare regulator in the State.

2. **Health (Amendment) (Home Support Providers) Bill 2025** - Introduces statutory registration and regulation of all home support providers. The Chief Inspector will be empowered to grant, amend, or revoke licences. Home support is a rapidly growing sector serving tens of thousands of people; this has never previously been regulated.

3. **EU Health Technology Assessment Regulation** - Requires HIQA to participate in joint European clinical assessments, increasing the international dimension of the HTA function.

### 3.4 Organisational Scale

| Metric | 2020 | 2023 | 2024 | Trajectory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Staff (WTE)** | ~300 | 364 | 417 | Growing rapidly; further growth required for new functions |
| **Budget** | ~€28m | €38.3m | ~€42m+ | Increasing in line with expanded remit |
| **Directorates** | 4 | 5 | 5+ | New functions being added |

### 3.5 Complexity and Risk Profile

The HIQA CEO role carries exceptional complexity and public accountability:

- **Life-and-death regulatory decisions**: HIQA regulates services for the most vulnerable people in society - older people in nursing homes, people with disabilities, children in care, and patients in hospitals
- **High public and political profile**: HIQA inspection reports attract significant media and Oireachtas attention; the CEO regularly appears before Oireachtas Committees
- **Enforcement powers**: Registration, conditions, warnings, cancellation of registration; future licensing powers for hospitals
- **COVID-19 legacy**: HIQA played a central role during the pandemic, particularly in nursing home oversight, and its role in public health emergency preparedness is now embedded
- **International engagement**: EU HTA Regulation, EUnetHTA, INAHTA membership
- **Multi-stakeholder environment**: Reports to HIQA Board, engages with Department of Health, HSE, private providers, patient advocacy groups, professional regulators, and international bodies

## 4. Benchmarking Against Comparable Domestic State Bodies

### 4.1 Comparator Table

| State Body | Head Title | Grade Equivalent | Staff | Budget | Regulatory Scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HIQA** | CEO | **Assistant Secretary** | **417** | **~€42m+** | **2,000+ registered centres; all public/private hospitals; HTA; home support (incoming)** |
| **Tusla** (Child & Family Agency) | CEO | **Deputy Secretary** | **5,700+** | **€1.3bn** | Child protection, family support, DSGBV, early years inspections |
| **EPA** (Environmental Protection Agency) | Director General | **Deputy Secretary** | **~450** | **~€100m+** | Environmental licensing, monitoring, enforcement; climate reporting |
| **CCPC** (Competition & Consumer Protection Commission) | Chairperson | **Deputy Secretary** | **~154** | **~€18m** | Competition law enforcement, consumer protection, mergers |
| **ComReg** (Commission for Communications Regulation) | Chairperson/Commissioner | **Deputy Secretary** | **~250** | **~€30m** | Telecoms, broadcasting, postal regulation |
| **CRU** (Commission for Regulation of Utilities) | Chairperson/Commissioner | **Deputy Secretary** | **~150** | **~€25m** | Energy, water, gas regulation |
| **An Coimisiun Pleanala** | Chairperson | **Deputy Secretary+** | **~200** | **~€25m** | Planning appeals, strategic infrastructure |
| **Mental Health Commission** | CEO | **Assistant Secretary** | **~80** | **~€14m** | Mental health service regulation, tribunals |
| **HSA** (Health & Safety Authority) | CEO | **Assistant Secretary** | **~200** | **~€25m** | Workplace health & safety regulation |

### 4.2 Key Comparisons

#### HIQA vs Tusla (CEO at Deputy Secretary - €211,000)

| Dimension | HIQA | Tusla |
|---|---|---|
| **Staff** | 417 | 5,700+ |
| **Budget** | ~€42m | €1.3bn |
| **Regulatory complexity** | **Higher** - multi-sector independent regulator with enforcement powers across health and social care | Service delivery agency with inspectorate function |
| **Public accountability** | **Higher** - independent regulator making life-safety decisions; inspection reports published; licensing authority (incoming) | High - child protection |
| **Grade** | Assistant Secretary | Deputy Secretary |

While Tusla is larger by headcount and budget, HIQA's role as an **independent regulator with enforcement and licensing powers** across the entire health and social care system carries a qualitatively different and arguably greater level of complexity, public exposure, and personal accountability for the CEO. The Tusla CEO is graded at Deputy Secretary.

#### HIQA vs EPA (Director General at Deputy Secretary - ~€219,258)

| Dimension | HIQA | EPA |
|---|---|---|
| **Staff** | 417 | ~450 |
| **Budget** | ~€42m | ~€100m+ |
| **Regulatory scope** | 2,000+ registered health/social care centres + all hospitals + HTA | Environmental licensing, monitoring, enforcement |
| **Services regulated** | Health and social care for most vulnerable populations | Industrial, waste, water |
| **Life-safety dimension** | **Direct** - nursing homes, hospitals, disability, children | Indirect - environmental/public health |
| **Grade** | Assistant Secretary | Deputy Secretary |

HIQA and the EPA are closely comparable in staff numbers. HIQA's regulatory scope (2,000+ facilities) arguably exceeds the EPA's. The direct life-safety nature of HIQA's regulatory decisions (nursing homes, hospitals, disability services) represents a higher-consequence regulatory environment. The EPA Director General is at Deputy Secretary.

#### HIQA vs CCPC/ComReg/CRU (Heads at Deputy Secretary)

The heads of the CCPC (~154 staff, ~€18m budget), ComReg (~250 staff), and CRU (~150 staff) are all remunerated at Deputy Secretary level or equivalent. Each of these bodies has **fewer staff, smaller budgets, and narrower regulatory scope** than HIQA. None carries direct responsibility for the safety of vulnerable people in residential or hospital care.

## 5. Criteria for Deputy Secretary Grading - How HIQA Meets Each

Based on the criteria used by the Review Body on Higher Remuneration and the Senior Posts Remuneration Committee (SPRC), the following factors determine the appropriate grading for state body CEO roles:

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **1. Scale of organisation** | 417 staff, ~€42m budget - comparable to or exceeding EPA, CCPC, ComReg, CRU |
| **2. Complexity of functions** | Multi-sector regulator: health, social care, HTA, health information. Unique combination of registration, inspection, enforcement, standards-setting, and technology assessment |
| **3. Scope of regulatory remit** | 2,000+ registered centres, all public/private hospitals, home support (incoming). Broadest health/social care regulatory remit in the State |
| **4. Impact and consequence of decisions** | Life-safety decisions affecting the most vulnerable. Registration/cancellation of nursing homes and disability centres. Hospital licensing (incoming) |
| **5. Public profile and accountability** | Very high. Regular Oireachtas Committee appearances. Published inspection reports attract national media attention. CEO personally identified with regulatory failures/successes |
| **6. Stakeholder complexity** | Board, Department of Health, HSE, private sector providers, patient groups, professional regulators, EU institutions |
| **7. Rate of change and growth** | Remit has expanded continuously since 2007 and is set for further major expansion (hospital licensing, home support, EU HTA) |
| **8. Recruitment and retention** | Assistant Secretary grade creates insufficient headroom from direct reports and limits the candidate pool for a role requiring exceptional regulatory, clinical governance, and leadership expertise |

## 6. Pay and Headroom Analysis

### 6.1 Current Pay Scales (PPC, 2025)

| Grade | Salary |
|---|---|
| **Principal Officer (higher)** | ~€107,000 - €125,000 |
| **Assistant Secretary** | ~€169,819 - €194,262 |
| **Deputy Secretary** | ~€219,258 |
| **Secretary General Level III** | ~€250,465 |

### 6.2 Headroom Problem

HIQA's senior management team includes Director-level posts at Principal Officer (Higher) and equivalent grades. At the Assistant Secretary rate, the CEO salary provides limited headroom above direct reports, particularly for directors on the upper end of the PO Higher scale or on the Assistant Secretary scale.

The SPRC's 2024 report explicitly identified **insufficient headroom between CEOs and their direct reports** as a systemic problem across the public service. This finding directly supports regrading.

### 6.3 Recruitment Risk

The HIQA CEO role demands a rare combination of:
- Senior healthcare management experience
- Regulatory and enforcement expertise
- Governance and public accountability experience
- Clinical governance knowledge
- Political and stakeholder management skills

At Assistant Secretary level, the salary is uncompetitive relative to comparable roles in the HSE, hospital groups, and private sector healthcare. Regrading to Deputy Secretary is essential to attract and retain candidates of the necessary calibre.

---

## 7. The Anomaly: Why HIQA is Under-Graded

The HIQA CEO grading at Assistant Secretary was set at HIQA's establishment in 2007. At that time:

- HIQA's regulatory remit was narrower (primarily nursing homes and some children's services)
- Staff numbered fewer than 200
- The budget was significantly smaller
- HIQA had no hospital oversight function
- The Patient Safety licensing framework did not exist
- The HTA function was nascent
- The EU HTA Regulation did not exist
- Home support services were unregulated

**The role in 2026 bears little resemblance to the role as conceived in 2007.** The grade has simply not been reviewed to reflect the transformation.

By contrast, bodies like the EPA, Tusla, CCPC, ComReg, and CRU have had their CEO grades set or adjusted at Deputy Secretary level, reflecting their scale and complexity. HIQA has been left behind despite comparable or greater growth in scope.

---

## 8. Summary of the Case

| Factor | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Comparable bodies at Deputy Secretary** | Tusla, EPA, CCPC, ComReg, CRU, An Coimisiun Pleanala - all at Deputy Secretary or above |
| **HIQA staff and budget** | 417 WTE, ~€42m - exceeds CCPC, ComReg, CRU; comparable to EPA |
| **Regulatory scope** | 2,000+ registered centres + all hospitals + home support (incoming) - **unmatched domestically** |
| **Life-safety dimension** | Directly regulates care of the most vulnerable - nursing homes, hospitals, disability, children |
| **Growth trajectory** | Continuous expansion since 2007; three major new functions imminent |
| **SPRC finding** | Insufficient headroom between CEO and direct reports identified as systemic issue |
| **Recruitment risk** | Assistant Secretary grade is uncompetitive for the calibre of candidate required |

---

## 9. Recommendation

The Board of HIQA should formally request, through the Department of Health and the Department of Public Expenditure, NDP Delivery and Reform, that the CEO post be regraded from **Assistant Secretary to Deputy Secretary** equivalent, effective immediately.

This regrading:

- Corrects a longstanding anomaly
- Aligns HIQA with comparable domestic state body CEO grades
- Reflects the actual scale, complexity, and consequence of the role
- Addresses the headroom gap identified by the SPRC
- Supports recruitment and retention of the highest-calibre leadership
- Recognises the imminent further expansion of HIQA's remit under the Patient Safety (Licensing) Bill and the Home Support Providers Bill

The incremental cost (~€25,000 - €49,000 per annum) is negligible in the context of HIQA's budget and is entirely justified by the scope, scale, and significance of the role.
